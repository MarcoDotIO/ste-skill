# Writing and review guide

## Selected Issue 9 rules

This short summary is incomplete. Consult the [official standard](https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf) for definitions and exceptions.

- Check dictionary words for approved meaning, part of speech, and form. Other vocabulary must qualify as technical nouns or technical verbs.
- Use consistent technical terms. Do not use technical nouns as verbs or technical verbs as nouns.
- Use imperatives for instructions. Put a prerequisite condition before its command. Separate actions, except actions that occur together.
- Limit procedure sentences to 20 words and descriptive sentences or notes to 25 words.
- Keep descriptive paragraphs to one topic and at most six sentences.
- Use active voice. In descriptions, passive voice is permitted when the actor is unknown.
- Keep noun groups to three words unless an allowed exception applies.
- Do not use semicolons. Check restricted verb forms, including uses of “-ing,” against the standard.
- Apply the standard's counting rules. Numbers with units count once. Parentheses count once in the outer sentence; check their contents separately. Quotations, names, identifiers, abbreviations, hyphenated expressions, and vertical lists need special treatment.

## Apply the rules to agent output

These are workflow choices for this skill, not additional ASD rules.

For a software guide, leave executable examples intact. A long command is not a reason to change a flag. Do not split a file path to fit a sentence limit.

For a report, keep claims linked to their evidence. A small simulated study does not establish field performance. Preserve qualifiers, sample sizes, confidence intervals, and citations.

For support text, describe what the user can see and do. Keep the product's actual labels. Explain unfamiliar terms at first use when the audience needs that explanation.

For a mixed document, review each passage by purpose. An installation step is procedural. An explanation of a system component is descriptive. Do not classify the whole document by its filename.

For warnings, retain the stated hazard, consequence, avoidance action, severity, and placement. If one is missing, do not fabricate it. Flag a consequential gap in the delivery note.

## Terminology record

Keep a record only as large as the task needs. Use this structure for disputed or specialist terms:

| Term | Meaning here | Part of speech | Evidence | Review status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Project term | Specific meaning | Noun or verb | Source, dictionary entry, or technical category | Verified or unresolved |

The row is a template. It does not approve the words in it. A familiar technical word is not automatically an accepted technical term. A local word list cannot waive a standard rule.

## Review order

1. Compare source and draft for facts, conditions, obligations, and protected text.
2. Review terminology in context. Check both dictionary entries and any claimed technical-term category.
3. Review sentence structure, paragraph structure, and word counts.
4. Review the document as the intended reader. Check that references and labels still point to the correct items.
5. Report only the review scope that you completed.
